Meet Our Adjudicators

Click an adjudicator below to learn more about their experience and background.

Luke Holder

Piano • Vocal • Percussion • Conductor • Theatre

Luke Holder is a distinguished South African musician, conductor, educator, and theatre practitioner, widely respected for his multifaceted expertise in music performance, music education, and production design. With over two decades of experience in the performing arts, his career reflects a seamless fusion of academic excellence, technical mastery, and creative innovation.

Holder's formal training in music began early, earning a Licentiate (LTCL) in Piano Performance, followed by an Associate (ATCL) in Organ Performance. His academic pursuits include a BA in Drama and Linguistics from the University of Natal, and an Honours degree in Applied Mathematics from UKZN, demonstrating an exceptional ability to integrate artistic and analytical disciplines.

A versatile and accomplished musician, Holder has served as a conductor and accompanist for both orchestral and choral ensembles, and has performed professionally in acclaimed productions such as My Fair Lady and Cinderella under Ralph Lawson. He has directed and musically led numerous large-scale theatrical productions including Man of La Mancha, Footloose, Into the Woods, and the South African premieres of High School Musical and its sequel.

His musical direction and compositional work have been commissioned internationally, notably by the Apelsinchik Cantabile Chamber Choir in Moscow, and his choral arrangements remain in permanent repertoire for ensembles such as the KZN Youth Choir and UCT Chamber Choir.

Holder's influence extends deeply into education. He has taught music, drama and mathematics at leading institutions including Kearsney College, Westville Boys' High School, and Hilton College, where he served as Director of Creative and Performing Arts.

He also lectured in Ethnomusicology at the prestigious Berklee College of Music in Boston, starting a department that is still an active part of the curricular offering at the College. His contributions to curriculum development and textbook authorship reflect his commitment to accessible, high-quality music and drama education across under-resourced contexts.

He has served as chief examiner for the DBE in both Dramatic Arts and Music, and was part of the original team that wrote the current CAPS documents for both subjects.

Beyond his musical qualifications, Holder is highly skilled in sound engineering, lighting design, and multimedia integration, with proficiency in industry-standard platforms such as QLab, Cubase, and Adobe Audition. His holistic understanding of the technical and artistic dimensions of performance allows him to realise complex productions with both finesse and vision.

Holder's work has earned him significant recognition, including an Honorary Award for Youth Development in Education, Performing Arts, Entrepreneurship, and Leadership at the KZN Young Achievers Awards in 2019. As an adjudicator, coach, and mentor, he continues to shape the next generation of performing artists, while actively contributing to the evolution of South Africa's cultural and artistic landscape.

Jennifer du Plessis

Wind • Piano • Theory • Trinity College London

Jennifer du Plessis is an experienced music educator with over twenty years of teaching experience in independent schools and private practice in KwaZulu-Natal. Her work focuses particularly on preparing pupils for international music examinations and school-based assessments, guiding learners to develop strong technical foundations, musical understanding, and confident performance skills.

Jennifer completed her schooling at The Wykeham Collegiate. She later completed the DipABRSM in 2006. Her teaching is supported by ongoing professional development in areas such as choral conducting, Orff education, and music pedagogy.

Throughout her career, Jennifer has prepared pupils for a wide range of graded and diploma-level examinations through Trinity College London and ABRSM. She has extensive experience guiding students through all aspects of exam preparation, including repertoire selection, technical development, musical interpretation, and performance readiness.

In addition, she assists learners preparing for school music assessments, including the practical and theory components of the Independent Examinations Board (IEB) matric music programme.

Jennifer has taught at several respected schools in Pietermaritzburg, including The Wykeham Collegiate, St Charles College, and Epworth School. Since 2003 she has also been associated with Hilton College, where she teaches clarinet, saxophone, piano and theory and supports pupils preparing for the practical component of IEB matric music.

From 2024 to 2026 she served as Head of Culture and Music teacher at Cordwalles Preparatory School. From April 2026 she will focus entirely on private music teaching.

In addition to her teaching work, Jennifer is a published author for Pearson Education and Shuter & Shooter, contributing performing arts content for South African Life Skills textbooks. She currently serves as the KwaZulu-Natal representative for Trinity College London, coordinating and facilitating music examinations across the region.

Jennifer is passionate about supporting young musicians in achieving their full potential, helping them succeed in examinations while fostering a lifelong appreciation for music.

Claire Mathews

Violin • Viola • Cello • String Ensembles

Claire Mathews is a violinist and cellist based in Hilton, KZN. One of five children, she grew up playing Celtic folk tunes and duets with her siblings, especially her older brother. Being home schooled provided the time and flexibility to dedicate all her energy to honing her skills as a young musician.

She received her Bachelor of Music through North West University, with a focus on violin methodology and teaching.

During her time at university, she garnered experience as a first violinist and cellist in the North-West Youth Orchestra, and as the concert master of the NWU Orchestra. In this time, she also started teaching at local schools and discovered a love for teaching music.

Claire now teaches violin, viola, and cello at several schools in Pietermaritzburg and Hilton, aiming to create a vibrant culture of young string players in the KZN Midlands.

While she pours most of her energy and passion into educating the next generation of string players, she enjoys sharing the joy of making music with others in a variety of settings.

She leads the Pietermaritzburg City Orchestra, takes part in informal and recreational ensembles, and conducts and manages the Hilton College and PMB GHS string ensembles.

Dr Rosalie Conrad

Choirs • Ensembles • Piano • Vocal

Dr Rosalie Conrad has BMus and MMus degrees from Pretoria University, and a DMus from the University of Durban-Westville on the chorus in Verdi's operas.

She was repetiteur at PACT before joining the music staff of UDW, where she lectured in theory, composition, orchestration, and choral studies and technique, and accompanied students on the piano and harpsichord.

Rosalie also directed the UDW Choir. She trained the NAPAC Chorus for many years, winning the Nederburg Opera prize for her direction of the chorus in Verdi's Nabucco.

She served as external examiner at various universities and for the Matriculation Board and she was on the UNISA music examining panel.

Rosalie adjudicates instrumental, vocal and choral competitions, and was a long-standing adjudicator for the National Choir Festival.

She is active in the South African Society of Music Teachers, having been National President three times.

Rosalie currently runs a private studio and directs the Durban Symphonic Choir, as well as teaching singing at Crawford North Coast and piano at Durban Girls' College.
